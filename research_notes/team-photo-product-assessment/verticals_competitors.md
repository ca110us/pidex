# Team Photo Documentation Software: Non-Hospital Verticals, Competitors, Willingness to Pay

Research date: 2026-10-19. Sources are mostly vendor pages, press, SEC filings and review aggregators. Aggregator revenue and pricing figures often disagree, and these notes say where they do.

## 1. CompanyCam: scale, revenue, funding, pricing, AI, acquisitions, limitations, why vertical worked

### Takeaway
CompanyCam is the reference company for this category. It is a contractor photo-documentation app, founded by a roofer in 2015, that reached a $2B valuation in August 2025 (PitchBook says the Series C was about $415M, led by B Capital). Confirmed ARR was over $20M in July 2022. Third-party 2024 estimates put it around $68M ARR, unconfirmed. Pricing is a per-company plan of $63–$199/month plus $29 per extra user. The product has grown from photos into job management, payments and marketing, and in March 2026 it bought Beam Finance, a contractor fintech.

### Cited Findings
- Valuation: $2B after the August 2025 Series C, per PitchBook, making it Nebraska's first unicorn. The company did not disclose the round size. PitchBook puts it at $415M, led by B Capital (co-founded by Eduardo Saverin). CompanyCam had raised $38M before this round, including a $30M Series B in 2021 led by Insight Partners. Other investors: Nelnet, JMI Equity, Blueprint Equity, WndrCo, Invest Nebraska — [Silicon Prairie News, Nov 2025](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/); [William Blair (advisor on the B Capital transaction)](https://www.williamblair.com/News/CompanyCam-and-B-Capital-Transaction)
- Origin: CEO Luke Hansen built the app in 2015 for his family's roofing business (White Castle Roofing). It launched on the App Store on July 1, 2015 — [Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/)
- Users: "hundreds of thousands of users worldwide." More than 21,000 App Store reviews at 4.8 stars. About 161 employees at the Lincoln HQ (Sept 2025) and more than 200 in total by 2022. A UK expansion is under way — [Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/)
- Revenue:
  - The only company-stated figure: ARR above $20M in July 2022 — [Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/)
  - GetLatka estimates $68M ARR for 2024 (from $32M in 2023) — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/companycam)
  - LeadIQ estimates $250–500M as of Aug 2026. This is an unreliable aggregator range — [LeadIQ](https://leadiq.com/c/companycam/5a1db7ba2300005400b363f1)
  - Other aggregators range from under $1M (Owler) to $48M (ZoomInfo), which are clearly unreliable — [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/companycam), [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/companycam-inc/369218079)
- AI: "AI actions" grew 300% year over year. On the roadmap: auto-generated reports, predictive workflows, "enhanced photo tagging and search." This suggests tagging and search were still weak points as of late 2025 — [Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/)
- Pricing (companycam.com/pricing, fetched Oct 2026; cards say "USD / Billed Annually"; annual billing saves 20%) — [CompanyCam Pricing](https://companycam.com/pricing):
  - **Core $63/mo**: 1 user included, max 2. Time-stamped photos and videos, project timeline, photo reports, offline sync, payments, price book and invoicing, 10 AI credits.
  - **Crew $129/mo**: 3 users included. Checklists, task assignment, live job map, client access, proposals from voice and photos, 100 AI credits.
  - **Scale $199/mo**: 3 users included. Unlimited templates and auto-reports, subcontractor permissions, camera room measurement, e-signature, auto-translation, unlimited AI credits.
  - **Enterprise**: custom pricing for 50+ users. SSO, multi-location hub, dedicated customer success manager.
  - Extra users cost $29 each. A Marketing Suite add-on (listed at $99/$79) covers AI social posts, Google review management and website gallery.
  - AI features named: AI Walkthrough, AI-Powered Documents, AI Checklists.
- Acquisition: in March 2026 CompanyCam bought Beam Finance, a California fintech making AI estimates, invoicing and expenses software for contractors. It is CompanyCam's first acquisition. Beam had raised an $11M Series A earlier in 2026, and both companies keep operating independently. (Not to be confused with Modern Treasury's 2025 purchase of a stablecoin company also called Beam.) — [Silicon Prairie News, Mar 2026](https://siliconprairienews.com/2026/03/companycam-acquires-california-construction-fintech-startup-beam/); [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/companycam-acquires-california-fintech-beam-after-11m-series-a-raise)
- Review limitations:
  - Search is "very limited… I wish there were an option for 'contains' or 'starts with'" — [Capterra reviews](https://capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/)
  - The tagging feature is "a bit unintuitive" — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.ca/software/126007/companycam)
  - Large photo sets are hard to tag — [Capterra p.2–4](https://www.capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/?page=2)
  - Photos lose quality after upload, and zoom has issues — [Capterra p.3](https://www.capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/?page=3)
  - Overall rating is 4.6 on Capterra (about 99 reviews in that snapshot) — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/)

### Inferences
- Why going vertical worked:
  - The founder knew the pain firsthand, from roofing.
  - The atomic unit is a job/project with an address and GPS, which maps exactly onto a "case."
  - Photos are proof of work for customers, insurers and disputes, which is direct ROI.
  - Contractors are a huge, fragmented SMB market that buys self-serve from the App Store, so bottom-up growth is possible.
  - Once photos are the system of record, it can sell up into checklists, reports, payments, marketing and fintech. Beam extends the wedge into money flows.
- Price anchor for a horizontal entrant: SMB teams already pay about $60–200/month per company (about $29/month per extra seat) for job-scoped photo capture and sharing. That is a proven willingness to pay, well above consumer photo storage.
- CompanyCam's own roadmap emphasises "enhanced photo tagging and search," and reviewers complain about search and tagging. AI tagging and semantic search are therefore a real gap even in the category leader. CompanyCam will likely close it, though, given its funding.
- CompanyCam is moving up-market and into an all-in-one contractor OS. That leaves room for a simpler, cheaper, photo-centric tool. It also leaves room in non-contractor verticals that CompanyCam's construction-specific features (room measurement, price book, proposals) do not serve.

### Gaps
- No company-confirmed 2024–2026 ARR or paying-customer count. Sacra had no accessible CompanyCam profile in search.
- The terms of the Beam deal were not disclosed.
- Could not confirm whether $63/$129/$199 are annual-billed monthly rates or month-to-month rates.

## 2. Other vertical photo / inspection tools: size and pricing

### Takeaway
Every vertical where photos act as evidence has its own paid tool. Construction/field service, auto repair (digital vehicle inspection, DVI), home inspection, property management and insurance claims all show clear willingness to pay. The pricing models differ:
- **Per user per month**: $20–55 (Fulcrum, SiteCapture, zInspector).
- **Per company per month**: $90–220 (Spectora, HomeGauge, AutoVitals, CompanyCam).
- **Per unit**: HappyCo, at about $1 per unit per month.
- **Share of project value**: OpenSpace.
- **Enterprise contracts**: insurance AI (Tractable, CCC).

Larger platforms such as ServiceTitan (about $961M revenue) include photos as one feature.

### Cited Findings
**Construction / field data**
- OpenSpace (360° site capture with AI): $102M Series D in March 2022 at a $902M valuation, led by PSP Growth with BlackRock, Alkeon, Menlo and JLL — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-03-02/proptech-startup-openspace-reaches-valuation-of-902-million); [OpenSpace](https://www.openspace.ai/news/proptech-startup-openspace-reaches-valuation-of-902-million/). One aggregator gives $913M after a $9M extension in August 2022 — [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/openspace). Tracxn reports $199M raised over 7 rounds — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/openspace/__NenwGlB71RDzagK7xOo-m-j_fH-Hpmp3qw_XH1dZvsg). It is reportedly priced as a percentage of construction project value, not per user (aggregator claim from search results, unverified).
- Fulcrum (field data collection forms with photos): per user, with a free trial. Professional is about $43/user/month and Elite is listed at $55 or $39 depending on source. Enterprise is custom, with a 5-user minimum per ToolRadar — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136604/Fulcrum/pricing/); [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/fulcrum/pricing); [TrustRadius ($46)](https://www.trustradius.com/products/fulcrum/pricing)
- SiteCapture (photo-centric field inspection): per user, about $20–30/user/month, rated 4.6/5 on 28 reviews — [GetApp UK](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2075435/sitecapture)
- Raken (daily reports, time cards, photos): the pricing page shows no public prices and is demo-only — [Raken pricing](https://www.rakenapp.com/pricing)
- ServiceTitan (field service OS for the trades, which includes job photos):
  - FY2026 (ended Jan 31, 2026) revenue $961.0M, up 24%.
  - About 10,800 active customers (those billed over $10k/year).
  - More than 2,000 customers with annualized billings over $100k as of April 2026.
  - FY2027 revenue guidance $1,139–1,144M.
  - Sources: [ServiceTitan 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000163882626000028/ttan-20260131.htm); [Q2 FY27 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001638826/000163882626000093/ttan-ex99_1.htm); [ServiceTitan IR](https://investors.servicetitan.com/news-releases/news-release-details/servicetitan-announces-fiscal-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal)

**Auto repair: digital vehicle inspection (DVI)**
- AutoVitals: the vendor FAQ says DVI starts at $219/month per shop and varies by shop size. Capterra says "contact vendor," with no free trial. FitGap's $29.99 figure conflicts with the vendor's. The DVI.X module has no built-in shop management or POS — [AutoVitals](https://www.autovitals.com/?p=8778); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/199983/The-Digital-Shop/); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/autovitals)
- Bolt On Technology: makes DVI software for auto repair. No public pricing found — [Dialectica](https://www.dialectica.io/origin/company/bolt-on-technology)

**Home inspection**
- Spectora: about $99–109/month for software only, or $999/year. Extra inspectors are about $89/month. A $2,199 "Jumpstart" package is available, and advanced features are per-inspection add-ons — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/157144/Spectora/pricing/); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/home-inspection-software-reviews/); [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/spectora/pricing)
- HomeGauge: about $49–89/month, plus a promo of $6.90/month for 3 months on HomeGauge ONE — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/157144/spectora/); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/spectora/pricing); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/home-inspection-software-reviews/)

**Property management inspections**
- HappyCo: reportedly about $1/unit/month (Property Suite), $7 per unit per walk for Due Diligence, and a 500-unit minimum. These are third-party figures. Capterra lists $89 — [RentCheck blog](https://www.getrentcheck.com/blog/happyco-is-it-the-right-property-management-and-inspection-platform-for-me); [SnapInspect](https://blog.snapinspect.com/property-inspection-software-compared/)
- zInspector: from $22/month per user, often billed per inspection. Popular with independent inspectors and small teams — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/176250/zInspector/alternatives/); [HappyCo comparison (vendor)](https://happy.co/comparisons/happyco-vs-zinspector)

**Insurance claims photo AI**
- Tractable (AI on smartphone photos of car and property damage): $65M Series E in 2023 led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2, with Insight and Georgian. An aggregator claims about $115M raised at a $1B valuation (low authority). Competitors are CCC Intelligent Solutions (dominant in US auto claims), Mitchell and Solera/Audatex — [Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/tractable-ai-raises-65m-in-series-e-funding-led-by-softbank-vision-fund-2-investment-to-power-next-generation-ai-for-instant-visual-assessments-within-the-automotive-and-property-ecosystems/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tractable)

### Inferences
- Insurance claims AI (Tractable, CCC, Snapsheet) and 360° construction capture (OpenSpace) are enterprise sales with high ACVs. They are not good first targets for a horizontal SMB product.
- DVI, home inspection and property inspection tools bundle photos into a structured report (inspection template, findings, customer-facing report) and connect it to revenue: upsold repairs in DVI, the inspection fee in home inspection, work orders in property management. Willingness to pay comes from that report-to-revenue link, not from the photo library itself.
- In most verticals, photo capture plus case filing is commoditised inside a larger vertical suite (ServiceTitan, Spectora, HappyCo). A horizontal entrant competes against "a feature in software they already pay for." It has to win either where no vertical suite exists or as a cheaper, better photo layer.

### Gaps
- No verified current data found on Snapsheet, CCC photo AI pricing, Hover or EagleView (insurance/roofing measurement), ezyVet photo features (veterinary), Movista or Repsly (retail audit), or Jobber photo features. Searches were limited and these were not covered.
- No 2024–2026 funding or valuation update found for OpenSpace or Tractable.
- Raken and Bolt On pricing are not public.

## 3. Horizontal / team AI photo libraries: where they fail for case-based work

### Takeaway
Horizontal tools fall into three groups:
- **Enterprise DAMs** (Bynder, Canto): built for marketing brand assets, priced at roughly $6k–75k a year, sales-led.
- **AI tagging libraries** (Tagbox at $400–600/month, Excire, CoreViz, Eagle): good at tagging and visual search but desktop- or marketing-oriented.
- **Consumer/cloud storage** (Google Photos, Dropbox, OneDrive/SharePoint): cheap, but not built around cases.

None combines mobile capture at the moment of work, an auto-filed subject/case record, a per-case timeline, field-team permissions and compliance or audit trails.

### Cited Findings
- Bynder: no public pricing and no self-serve. Vendr median contract is about $37,109/year (range about $13.5k–74.6k across 62 purchases). Pricing is modular, and implementation costs $10k–50k+ — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/bynder/pricing). A competitor-authored page puts the median at about $41.4k — [Jam](https://jamcontent.com/comparison/bynder-alternatives)
- Canto: four tiers, no public prices. Third-party estimate of about $6k–12k/year at entry. Has visual search and facial recognition — [Jam](https://jamcontent.com/comparison/canto-alternatives); [Fabric](https://fabric.so/comparison/canto-vs-bynder)
- Tagbox.io: Basic about $400/month (up to 10k assets, unlimited users), Pro about $600/month, Enterprise custom. Features semantic visual search, face recognition, custom AI tags and logo detection. Targets e-commerce, events, agencies and hospitality — [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/software/tagbox-io)
- Excire Foto / Office: local, on-device AI keywording with prompt search and face search. Pricing conflicts: a $199 perpetual license per [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/excire-foto/about), or $60/user/month (Office) per [SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/excire-foto-office-edition)
- CoreViz Studio: AI media workspace with natural-language search (RAG) and similarity or duplicate search, plus an SDK and API. Has a free plan. Paid prices not found — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/coreviz-studio)

### Inferences
- DAMs organise around assets and campaigns, not around a subject such as a patient, vehicle, property, job or claim, and they are not built for capture. Their price and sales-led model rule out SMB field teams.
- AI tagging libraries (Tagbox, Excire, Eagle) solve "find the photo" but not "capture it on site and file it to job #123 with location, time and who took it." They also lack the case timeline and shareable report or client link that vertical tools sell on.
- Consumer and cloud tools (Google Photos shared albums, iCloud shared libraries, Dropbox/OneDrive) are the real incumbent for many SMBs: "free" and already installed. Their failure modes for case work are:
  - Photos mix with personal pictures.
  - No structured case metadata.
  - Weak role-based access, and no audit trail or immutable timestamps for compliance or disputes.
  - When an employee leaves, their photos stay on their device.
  - No reports.
  - This list is inferred from the product category. No primary source was fetched in this session.
- So the white space for a horizontal "photo case management" product is a CompanyCam-like capture-and-file workflow, made vertical-agnostic, with AI tagging and search built in. Pricing should sit near CompanyCam's ($30–60 per seat, or $60–200 per team) rather than DAM pricing.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve primary documentation on Google Photos shared library limits, Microsoft SharePoint/OneDrive AI image tagging, Dropbox AI search, or Eagle app pricing. Searches did not return them within the tool-call budget.

Public-company revenue (ServiceTitan's $961M) and private valuations (CompanyCam $2B, OpenSpace about $0.9B) are better evidence of value.

### Cited Findings
- Digital vehicle inspection software: about $4.9B in 2024, projected to about $10.6B by 2033 (8.1% CAGR). North America holds roughly 35–40% — [DataHorizzon Research](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/digital-vehicle-inspection-software-market-44806). The figure is suspiciously high relative to observable vendors (AutoVitals at about $219/month per shop), so it likely includes hardware and adjacent categories.
- Home inspection software: about $149M (2025) to about $478M (2035), with an internally inconsistent CAGR (10% vs 12.37%) — [Spherical Insights](https://www.sphericalinsights.com/blogs/top-25-companies-in-global-home-inspection-software-market-statistics-report-till-2035). - Broad "digital inspection" market (industrial included): $16.3B (2024) to $30.47B (2033), 7.2% CAGR. Not comparable to the niches above — [GII Korea](https://www.giikorea.co.kr/report/sky1950913-digital-inspection-market-size-share-growth.html)
- Value proxies:
  - ServiceTitan FY2026 revenue $961.0M — [SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000163882626000028/ttan-20260131.htm)

## 5. Ranking verticals: pain × willingness to pay × ease of SMB self-serve selling (for a new entrant)

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, the best targets for a new horizontal "photo case management" entrant are under-served photo-heavy SMB service niches that have no dominant vertical suite. Examples: specialty trades outside CompanyCam's core, restoration/cleaning, pest control, landscaping, equipment rental check-in/out, and small property managers. The entrant would lead with AI search and tagging plus case timelines. Directly attacking contractors (CompanyCam), auto repair (DVI suites) or home inspection (Spectora) means competing with entrenched, report-centric vertical incumbents. Insurance claims and 360° construction capture are enterprise sales and poor fits.

### Cited Findings (evidence underlying the ranking)
- Self-serve SMB willingness to pay is proven at $63–199/month per team plus $29/seat — [CompanyCam Pricing](https://companycam.com/pricing). Tools priced per user at $20–55/month also sell — [Fulcrum/Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136604/Fulcrum/pricing/), [SiteCapture/GetApp](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2075435/sitecapture)
- Search and tagging are acknowledged pain points even at the leader — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/reviews/), [Silicon Prairie News roadmap](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/11/companycam-reaches-unicorn-status-with-2-billion-valuation-a-first-for-a-nebraska-startup/)
- Enterprise-only or sales-led categories: Bynder (median about $37k/year) — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/bynder/pricing); OpenSpace (priced on project value) and Tractable (insurer contracts) — [Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/tractable-ai-raises-65m-in-series-e-funding-led-by-softbank-vision-fund-2-investment-to-power-next-generation-ai-for-instant-visual-assessments-within-the-automotive-and-property-ecosystems/)
- Vertical suites that bundle photos: ServiceTitan (10,800 customers at more than $10k/year) — [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000163882626000028/ttan-20260131.htm); Spectora and HomeGauge — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/157144/Spectora/pricing/); HappyCo — [SnapInspect](https://blog.snapinspect.com/property-inspection-software-compared/)

### Inferences (ranking is analyst judgment, not sourced fact)
| Vertical | Pain (photos as evidence) | Willingness to pay (observed) | SMB self-serve ease | Incumbent strength | Net attractiveness for a new horizontal entrant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specialty trades / restoration / cleaning / pest / landscaping (outside CompanyCam's roofing and general contractor core) | High (proof of work, disputes, before/after) | Medium-high (CompanyCam-style $60–200/month) | High | Medium (CompanyCam expanding; Jobber/ServiceTitan features) | **High**, but CompanyCam is the main threat |
| Small property management / landlord move-in and move-out | High (deposit disputes) | Medium ($1/unit; zInspector $22/month) | High for small landlords | Medium (HappyCo targets mid-size and large multifamily portfolios) | **High–Medium** |
| Equipment rental / fleet check-in and check-out damage | High | Medium | Medium-high | Low–medium (not researched) | **Medium–High** (needs validation) |
| Home inspection | High | Medium ($50–110/month) | High | High (Spectora, HomeGauge; report-centric) | Medium–Low |
| Auto repair DVI | High (photos sell repairs) | High ($219+/month per shop) | Medium | High (AutoVitals, Bolt On, shop management integrations) | Low–Medium |
| Construction general contractors / roofing | High | High | High | Very high (CompanyCam $2B, OpenSpace, Raken, Procore) | Low (head-on) |
| Insurance claims | High | Very high (enterprise) | Low | Very high (CCC, Tractable, Mitchell) | Low |
| Marketing / brand asset libraries | Low–Medium | High (enterprise DAM) | Low | High (Bynder, Canto) | Low (wrong job-to-be-done) |

- Strategic implication: CompanyCam itself shows that going vertical wins at go-to-market, through word of mouth within a trade, trade-specific templates and reports, and payments. A horizontal product may need a horizontal core plus vertical "skins" (templates, report formats, terminology) and should launch in one or two beachhead niches rather than selling as "for everyone."

### Gaps
- Pain and willingness-to-pay scores for restoration, pest control, equipment rental, veterinary and retail audit are inferred, not backed by evidence gathered in this session. Retail audit (Movista, Repsly) and veterinary (ezyVet) were not researched within the tool budget.
- No survey data found on SMB photo-management behaviour or switching costs.
