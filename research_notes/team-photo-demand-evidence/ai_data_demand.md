# Published demand for expert-labeled, consented real-world domain photos (clinical + field) for AI training

Verification legend (applies to every item):
- **[V1 fetched]**: I fetched the page and confirmed the quote/fact. Quotes came back through a fetch summarizer, so wording is near-verbatim. Re-check punctuation before publishing.
- **[V2 snippet]**: seen only in search-engine result summaries. I did not fetch the page, or the fetch failed (404/403/JS-only). Treat as a lead, not a confirmed fact.
- Research date: 2026-10-19. China-based sources excluded.

Overall caveat: I found **no public buyer-side request posts** (Datarade buyer requests, Reddit r/datasets "looking for…", Kaggle discussions, HN) for wound, skin, roof, or construction-defect photos. `site:reddit.com` and Kaggle searches returned only datasets, never request threads. Most of the evidence below is **indirect demand**: paid expert-labeling gigs, data-licensing marketplaces raising money, research groups saying data is scarce, and open calls asking for contributions. It is not a buyer publishing a purchase order.

## 1. Data buyer requests on marketplaces and forums (Datarade, Defined.ai, AWS/Snowflake, Reddit, Kaggle, HN, LinkedIn)

### Takeaway
I could not find or verify any public buyer request with a stated budget for consented, expert-labeled clinical or field photos. Marketplaces show the **supply side**: sellers list car-damage, construction-site, and wound/skin image sets with prices. That means vendors think buyers exist, but the buyers themselves stay private. The strongest demand-side signal is that venture-funded "governed data marketplaces" (Protege) say they are growing fast on licensed, rights-cleared medical imaging.

### Cited Findings
- **Protege (NY, founded 2024), data-licensing marketplace.** Aug 17, 2025: $25M Series A led by Footwork. Reports "over 100 data partners" across healthcare and media and a catalog with "hundreds of millions of medical images." CEO Bobby Samuels: "Access to the right training data continues to be the biggest bottleneck to AI's progress." Investor Nikhil Basu Trivedi (Footwork): "As more organizations look to build AI products grounded in real-world data, Protege's platform will be critical." No revenue figures; the article says "significant revenue" since seed. [V1 fetched] — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/protege-25-million-series-a-raised-for-expanding-ai-training-data-platform/)
- **Protege, Jan 8, 2026: $30M Series A extension led by a16z.** The marketplace connects "organizations that hold valuable data with AI developers who need it for training and evaluation". Supply includes "de-identified clinical health records and medical imaging". It emphasizes "rights-protected access to real-world data", "ensuring compliance with privacy and intellectual property requirements", and that it will "compensate data owners", with data partners receiving revenue share "with each use". Partner network expanded "to hundreds of organizations." [V1 fetched] — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/08/protege-raises-30m-grow-governed-marketplace-ai-training-data/). A claim of "20x" business growth in 2025 appeared only in search summaries [V2 snippet] — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/protege-raises-30m-series-a-extension-led-by-andreessen-horowitz-to-expand-access-to-real-world-data-for-ai)
- **Datarade (supply side, car damage).** TagX lists a car-damage image dataset (200,000+ annotated images, bounding box/polygon), "one-off purchases starting at $50,000," according to search summaries [V2 snippet]. When I fetched the page it showed only the TagX provider profile ("Contact TagX to obtain current pricing") [V1 fetched, price NOT confirmed] — [Datarade/TagX](https://datarade.ai/data-products/image-dataset-of-cars-with-highlighted-damages-tagx). Pixta AI also lists damaged-car image sets [V2 snippet] — [Datarade/Pixta](https://datarade.ai/data-products/3-000-damaged-car-images-for-ai-ml-model-pixta-ai)

- **Defined.ai (supply side, clinical).** "Medical Imagery – Wound & Skin Image Dataset": about 100,800 images, field "Healthcare/Sensitive Images" [V2 snippet; the page is JS-rendered and the fetch returned "Loading…"] — [Defined.ai](https://defined.ai/datasets/wound-skin-image)
- **Gradient Health, Nov 2025.** Its Atlas de-identified medical imaging platform became available on Google Cloud Marketplace for AI developers. This is radiology, not photos [V2 snippet] — found via search, cited in [Radiology Business context](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/health-it/enterprise-imaging/dicom-de-identifies-53-million-medical-imaging-exams)
- **Hospital for Special Surgery / Dicom Systems.** About 5.3M MSK imaging exams were de-identified and delivered to an AI partner working on fracture misdiagnosis. No price disclosed. This is radiology rather than photography, but it shows the hospital-to-AI data flow pattern [V2 snippet] — [Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/health-it/enterprise-imaging/dicom-de-identifies-53-million-medical-imaging-exams)
- **Forum "ask" (car damage).** A DataRobot community post reportedly said it was hard to find public car-damage datasets and pointed to Kaggle [V2 snippet; the community site now redirects to DataRobot docs, so the original thread is gone] — [DataRobot community (dead link)](https://community.datarobot.com/t5/ai-platform-trial/visual-ai-to-detect-the-damage-on-car-with-border-and-labels/m-p/8262)
- **Consumer "sell your photos for AI" apps.** Kled (iOS) is described in search summaries as "the first app that rewards you for your photos and videos," licensing uploads for AI training [V2 snippet; the App Store fetch failed] — [App Store: Kled](https://apps.apple.com/app/kled/id6752585718). Microsoft Garage "Trove" (2020) paid U.S. users for photos to train AI [V2 snippet] — [Microsoft Garage blog](https://microsoft.com/en-us/garage/blog/2020/09/get-paid-for-your-photos-with-trove-a-microsoft-garage-project)

### Inferences
- Buyers of this kind of data (insurers, insurtech, medtech AI, frontier labs) buy privately, through vendors (Protege, Defined.ai, TagX, Aya Data) or direct partnerships. They don't post public RFPs. So "no public request found" does not mean "no demand." It does mean that a new supplier would need to sell through brokers or BD, not respond to open requests.
- Protege's pitch matches the proposed app's value proposition almost exactly: rights-cleared, provenance-tracked, revenue share to the data owner. That makes it a plausible distribution channel for a clinic or contractor that produces consented, labeled photos.

### Gaps
- No verifiable Datarade "buyer request," AWS Data Exchange or Snowflake request, Reddit r/datasets / r/computervision "looking for wound/roof damage photos" thread, Kaggle discussion, or HN "Ask HN" post was found. Searches returned datasets, not requests.
- No public prices from buyers. The only price points are seller list prices (TagX $50k, unconfirmed) and labor rates (Section 2).
- LinkedIn posts by startups seeking data partners can't be searched with these tools.

## 2. Job postings and gigs asking doctors or inspectors to label or supply photos

### Takeaway
This is the **strongest and most concrete demand evidence**. Several AI-training labor platforms (Turing, Mercor, Outlier, Ryz Labs, HumanSignal) have recruited dermatologists to label skin-lesion images and auto-claims people to mark vehicle-damage photos for a European insurer. Pay runs from about €11/hr for damage marking to $120–$270/hr for dermatologists. Expert labeling of domain photos is clearly paid for. Photo **supply** (as opposed to labeling) is not what these gigs ask for.

### Cited Findings
- **Turing, "Medical Expert – Dermatology".** "We are hiring detail-oriented Dermatology Doctors to support large-scale dermatology image labeling projects." The task involves "drawing precise bounding boxes around lesions in accordance with strict medical-grade guidelines." Requirements: "MD/DNB in Dermatology", "5+ years of post-residency clinical experience", "Comfort working with sensitive or graphic medical images". No pay listed or date shown. [V1 fetched] — [Turing](https://work.turing.com/r/VUQcQRNXwX)
- **HumanSignal (Label Studio maker), "AI Trainer – Automotive Expertise Required".** The project is for "a leading European insurance company". The work involves "Reviewing photos of damaged vehicles and marking the affected areas" and "identifying which part of the car is involved". Pay is "roughly €11/hour". "Must be based in the EU". Preferred background: "Auto insurance claims or appraisal", "Auto body or mechanic work". [V1 fetched] — [Greenhouse/HumanSignal](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/humansignal/jobs/6006504004) (a duplicate posting exists: [6006505004](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/humansignal/jobs/6006505004))
- **Mercor, "Dermatologist (MD) — Clinical Image Interpretation & AI Evaluation".** Search summaries described $270/hr remote contract work "reviewing skin-lesion images and describing attributes such as color, shape, morphology, and distribution" [V2 snippet]. When fetched (Oct 2026), the listing was **closed** and redirected to a generic "Medical Expert (M.D./D.O.)" pool at "$115-$150 / hr". Its stated purpose: "Your standards and expertise become the rubric that frontier AI is graded against." [V1 fetched for the current state; the $270 and image-task wording are unconfirmed] — [Mercor](https://work.mercor.com/jobs/list_AAABoIoJzIgiY9y4XW5Gc4m7)
- **Mercor, "Licensed Dermatologists, Mexico".** Search summary: $75–$80/hr for "evaluating and annotating dermatological images for AI models" [V2 snippet]. The fetched page is now closed and shows a generic $115–150/hr Medical Expert pool [V1 fetched, closed] — [Mercor](https://work.mercor.com/jobs/list_AAABmkexEXdz7_-0YTZPO7sl/licensed-dermatologists-mexico). Also "Dermatologist, U.S. Licensed", closed, "Posted a month ago", $115–150/hr [V1 fetched] — [Mercor](https://work.mercor.com/jobs/list_AAABoIenqBTItMDkaq5J6qF3/dermatologist-u-s-licensed)
- **Outlier (Scale AI's expert platform), dermatologist recruiting pages.** "Dermatologists Wanted to Train Frontier AI — Up to $120/hr, Remote, Flexible". Radiologist pages advertise up to $150, $200, or $250/hr. The radiology task is described as critiquing AI reads of MRI/CT [V2 snippet; the dermatologist page fetch returned 404] — [Outlier dermatologist](https://outlier.ai/experts/dermatologist); [Outlier dermatology](https://outlier.ai/experts/dermatology); [Outlier radiology $250](https://outlier.ai/experts/radiology-t9d6e1)
- **Ryz Labs, "Dermatologists - AI Trainer".** Requires U.S.-licensed MDs with 5+ years of practice, according to search summaries [V2 snippet; the Lever page now returns 404, so the posting is likely filled or closed] — [Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/RyzLabs/68268383-43bd-4ca6-9cd3-5c8457128bfa); [Rise mirror](https://us.joinrise.co/jobs/ryz-labs-dermatologists-ai-trainer-97me)
- **Aya Data (annotation vendor) case study.** It gathered and labeled 85,000 images of damaged vehicles for a client [V2 snippet] — [ayadata.ai](https://ayadata.ai/?p=18434)
- **Property damage.** I found **no** remote gig specifically for adjusters labeling property or roof damage photos. Only freelancer profiles mention it [V2 snippet] — [OpenTrain profile](https://www.opentrain.ai/profile/hezron-l)

### Inferences
- Demand for **expert judgment on images** is proven and well paid, especially dermatology. Rates of $75–$270/hr imply buyers value an expert label on a clinical photo highly. A workflow app that captures the clinician's label at point of care (where it is effectively free) could undercut that cost. That is an inference, not a cited claim.
- Several dermatology listings were closed or recycled into generic pools by Oct 2026. Demand may be project-based and bursty, and shifting toward text-based "AI evaluation" (vignettes, rubric grading) rather than image annotation.
- Vehicle damage labeling pays low (€11/hr), which suggests buyers treat it as semi-skilled work. Margins for vehicle-damage data supply are likely thin compared with clinical data.

### Gaps
- No verified posting that pays clinicians or inspectors to **supply** their own photos, as opposed to labeling someone else's.
- No property or roof damage, construction defect, or ortho photo labeling gig found.
- Dates on Mercor pages are relative ("Posted a month ago"), and the $270/hr figure is unconfirmed.

## 3. Research calls for data: papers and challenges citing a lack of consented, diverse real-world photo datasets

### Takeaway
The academic literature consistently documents scarcity of consented, diverse, well-labeled real-world clinical photos. This holds for dermatology (skin tone, everyday conditions, metadata) and for wounds (small datasets, poor tissue labels, privacy barriers). There are also standing or open **calls for contributions** in dermatology (ISIC), construction (Ruhr-Uni Bochum), and utility inspection (EPRI). Google paid for search ads to crowdsource consented skin photos, a direct revealed preference for consented, real-world, expert-labeled images.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Research SCIN blog, Mar 19, 2024.** "existing dermatology image datasets often lack representation of everyday conditions" (e.g., rashes, allergies, infections) and "skew towards lighter skin tones". Also, "race and ethnicity information is frequently missing, hindering our ability to assess disparities." On SCIN itself: "All contributions were made voluntarily with informed consent", and the method "uses advertisements on web search result pages". The dataset "currently contains over 10,000 images of skin, nail, or hair conditions". "One to three dermatologists labeled each contribution with up to five dermatology conditions" (labels "are not equivalent to a clinical diagnosis"). [V1 fetched] — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/scin-a-new-resource-for-representative-dermatology-images/); data at [GitHub](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin)
- **Annals of Family Medicine 2024 abstract (Jeong, Schaekermann, Lin), "Crowdsourcing Dermatology Images with Google Search Ads: Creating a Real-World Skin Condition Dataset for AI Development".** "Traditional clinical health datasets often fail to represent the diversity and breadth of real-world disease, influencing research and AI tools developed on those datasets." Images were collected "with informed consent, between March and November 2023". Results: 10,408 images from 5,033 U.S. contributors, a median of 22 submissions/day, and over 97.5% usable. Cost per image is not reported. [V1 fetched] — [Ann Fam Med](https://www.annfammed.org/content/22/supplement_1/6186)
- **Wen et al., Lancet Digital Health, published online Nov 9, 2021.** Systematic review of 21 open-access skin lesion datasets (106,950 images). Ethnicity and Fitzpatrick type were available for only 1.3% and 2.1% of images. "only 10 images were from individuals with Fitzpatrick skin type V, and only a single image was from an individual with Fitzpatrick skin type VI." Of 1,585 images with ethnicity data, none were from African, Afro-Caribbean, or South Asian backgrounds. The authors call for quality standards for metadata reporting. [V2 snippet; PDF not fetched] — [Univ. of Birmingham repository](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/characteristics-of-publicly-available-skin-cancer-image-datasets-); [PDF](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/155188609/1_s2.0_S2589750021002521_main.pdf); [The Hospitalist coverage](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/ai-skin-color-underrepresented-datasets-used-identify-skin-cancer)
- **Daneshjou et al. 2022 (Science Advances), Diverse Dermatology Images (DDI).** The authors created DDI because the first publicly available, expertly curated, pathologically confirmed image dataset with diverse skin tones did not yet exist. It has 656 images (208 FST I–II, 241 III–IV, 207 V–VI), retrospectively selected from Stanford pathology reports for 2010–2020. [V2 snippet] — [PubMed 35960806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35960806/); [Stanford AIMI](https://aimi.stanford.edu/datasets/ddi-diverse-dermatology-images)
- **SkinCon paper (2023).** PH2 and derm7pt "lack dark skin tones", and dermatology AI datasets "have traditionally lacked diverse skin tones". [V2 snippet] — [arXiv 2302.00785](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00785)
- **DermaCon-IN, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks.** A prospectively curated set of 5,450 clinical images from 2,993 patients at South Indian outpatient clinics. It argues existing data underrepresents outpatient, non-Western populations. [V2 snippet] — [NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a2c548b2ed3141400d86a235eaa6fad0-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)
- **ISIC Archive standing call for contributions.** "Contribute to the future of dermatologic care by sharing your dermatologic images with the ISIC Archive." It holds nearly 500,000 public images and welcomes contributions for research, AI development, or "simply to present a rare case encountered in clinical practice!" Requirements: accept the ISIC Terms of Use, choose a Creative Commons license per cohort, and supply a metadata CSV using the ISIC template and data dictionary. An optional embargo of up to 1 year is available. **Unpaid.** [V1 fetched] — [ISIC contribute](https://isic-archive.com/contribute)
- **Wound imaging, FUSeg challenge paper.** Existing public wound image datasets are small and poorly annotated (DFU Challenge, Medetec, WoundDB). Its own release has 1,210 annotated foot ulcer images from 889 patients. [V2 snippet] — [Tecnalia](https://scienceportal.tecnalia.com/en/publications/fuseg-the-foot-ulcer-segmentation-challenge/)
- **Wound tissue segmentation (arXiv 2025).** The field lacks publicly labelled datasets and standardized benchmarks; the paper introduces a 147-image, 6-tissue-class dataset. [V2 snippet] — [arXiv 2502.10652](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10652)
- **Chronic wound segmentation (arXiv 2410.03359).** Most public chronic wound datasets come from lighter-skinned patients, and the few with darker skin tones don't quantify them. [V2 snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03359)
- **Wound tissue labels (PMC9858639).** Most public datasets label only the overall wound area, not granulation, necrosis, or epithelialization. [V2 snippet] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858639/)
- **Syn3DWound (arXiv 2311.15836).** A simulated wound dataset built because real annotated wound data is scarce. [V2 snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15836v1)
- **SurgWound-Bench (arXiv 2508.15189, 2025).** 686–697 surgical wound images **scraped from social media** (Reddit, Twitter, Facebook, Instagram, RedNote) and labeled by Ohio State Wexner surgeons. This shows researchers resort to unconsented web images for lack of consented clinical sets. [V2 snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15189)
- **Ruhr-Universität Bochum, Chair of Computing in Engineering: open call to construction companies, Jun 7, 2022 (updated Apr 5, 2023).** "Dataset creation for the detection of formwork on construction sites". It asks for photos of formwork being transported, assembled, or disassembled, at minimum 1280×720, from about 10–100 m, with formwork filling at least ¼ of the frame, in varied times and weather, and no identifiable faces "unless the person has explicitly consented." No compensation and no licensing terms are stated. [V1 fetched] — [RUB call](https://www.inf.bi.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/iib/aktuelles/akt00500.html.en)
- **Construction honeycomb-defect dataset (Kuhnke et al.).** The authors found "web images do not capture the complete variance found in real-case scenarios". [V2 snippet] — [arXiv 2208.02313](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.02313)
- **EPRI (U.S. utility research institute), transmission inspection imagery.** The Transmission H-frame Dataset 1.0 has 12,158 drone images, of which 11,483 are labeled (8,038 for training, 3,445 withheld). It was released via an IEEE DataPort competition (Jul–Oct 2022). Rationale: "EPRI is addressing the data gap by creating and sharing datasets to support research and commercial innovation." [V1 fetched] — [IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/competitions/epri-electric-transmission-imagery)
- **EPRI Journal (undated, URL paths suggest about Jan 2020).** "Larger image data sets are needed to train the technologies and improve their effectiveness." EPRI's Dexter Lewis says the research "requires collaboration among utilities to pool and curate data". Nine ML technologies performed "well above random prediction but well below human analysis." [V1 fetched] — [EPRI Journal](https://eprijournal.com/can-transmission-line-inspection-be-completely-automated/). EPRI's distribution set (about 30,000 images, PII and EXIF scrubbed, "contributed from utility collaboration") [V2 snippet] — [EPRI T&D AI](https://distribution.epri.com/ai/)

### Inferences
- The scarcity is well documented and long-running (2020–2025). The stated **missing ingredients** match what a workflow capture app could add: consent at capture, skin-tone and other metadata, expert labels, standardized capture. ISIC and the Bochum call give concrete spec templates (metadata CSV, CC license, resolution and distance rules).
- Most research calls offer **no payment**. ISIC, Bochum, and EPRI all ask for donations or collaboration. Academic demand is real but mostly non-revenue. Revenue sits with commercial AI developers via brokers (Section 1) and labeling vendors (Section 2).
- Google spent ad money to crowdsource consented skin photos instead of buying them. This suggests that, in 2023, no supplier offered consented, diverse, real-world skin photos at scale.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve formal grant or consortium funding calls (e.g., NIH Bridge2AI, EU Horizon) specifically for clinical photo contribution.
- No ortho (orthopedic clinical photo) scarcity paper found in this pass.
- The consent status of ISIC and DDI images was not verified.

## 4. Companies whose business is collecting such data at point of capture, and their public statements about demand

### Takeaway
There are real precedents for "workflow capture produces a proprietary training corpus." TRUE-See accumulated 2M+ standardized medical photos and has partnered with UC Irvine to build AI from them. Tractable's damage-AI library was built from photos and estimates supplied by Mitchell and top U.S./European insurers. Smartvid.io's site-photo library was part of an Autodesk investment deal. None of these disclose prices paid per image.

### Cited Findings
- **Tractable (vehicle damage AI), Oct 2017.** CCO Adrien Cohen said "a big part" of the photo library came from Mitchell (2016 partnership). "But several top carriers from the U.S. and Western Europe also supplied photos along with corresponding estimates." The system had seen "hundreds of millions of images and the corresponding estimates", "And more images and estimates come in all the time." [V1 fetched] — [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2017/10/09/top-u-s-insurers-using-tractable-in-photo-estimating-ai-pilots/). Insurer customers include Ageas, The Hartford (2021), Admiral Seguros, and Tokio Marine [V2 snippet] — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/tractable-partners-with-the-hartford-to-accelerate-claims-processing-with-ai/)
- **TRUE-See Systems (color-accurate medical photography for wound care, dermatology, and plastics), Sep 17, 2025.** A strategic partnership with Dr. Peter D. Chang and UC Irvine's Center for Applied AI Research (A2IR) will use TRUE-See's "growing proprietary database of more than 2 million color-accurate medical photographs" to build AI clinical decision support for wound care, dermatology, plastic surgery, and more. MobiHealthNews says the company is raising capital. Altera Digital Health resells it as "Sunrise Medical Photography powered by TRUE-See." [V2 snippet; MobiHealthNews returned 403; the figures are the company's own claims] — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/true-see-teams-uc-irvine-ai-medical-photographs); [press release via santelog](https://www.santelog.com/actualites-sante-nasdaq/true-see-announces-strategic-partnership-dr-peter-chang-and-center-applied); [Altera](https://www.alterahealth.com/newsroom/altera-digital-health-introduces-sunrise-medical-photography-powered-by-true-see/)
- **Tissue Analytics × Wound Care Advantage.** WCA brings "17 years of clinical data" combined with AI and 3D imaging [V2 snippet] — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/wound-care-advantage-partners-with-tissue-analytics-combining-17-years-of-clinical-data-with-ai-and-3d-imaging-to-improve-patient-outcomes)
- **Smartvid.io × Autodesk (2017).** Autodesk's strategic investment came with access to Smartvid's large library of construction-site images [V2 snippet] — [ENR](https://enr.com/articles/print/42335-strategic-partnership-with-autodesk-comes-with-money-and-a-data-trove)
- **Protege (see Section 1).** Its business model is licensing from data holders, with "revenue share payouts with each use", the closest analog to monetizing a capture app's output. [V1 fetched] — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/08/protege-raises-30m-grow-governed-marketplace-ai-training-data/)

### Inferences
- The pattern repeats: the company that owns the **capture workflow** (estimating software, a medical photo system, site-photo software) ends up holding a valuable labeled corpus. Monetization tends to come from partnerships, investment, or in-house AI rather than per-image sales. A photo-workflow app could follow the same path, but the evidence points to partnership or licensing deals, not a liquid per-image market.
- Tractable's corpus came paired with **outcome labels** (repair estimates) generated by the existing workflow. Demand is for photo plus expert decision, which supports the "expert-labeled at capture" thesis.

### Gaps
- No public per-image or per-dataset prices from Tractable, TRUE-See, Smartvid, or insurer data deals.
- No 2024–2026 statements found about insurers selling or licensing property-damage photo archives.
- I could not confirm whether TRUE-See's photos carry patient consent for AI secondary use.
